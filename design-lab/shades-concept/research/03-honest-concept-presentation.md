# Honest Concept Hardware Presentation

How concept-stage and research hardware is presented without pretending it exists.

## Observations

1. **Kickstarter's rendering rule (2012)**: Photorealistic product simulations prohibited; sketches, CAD, prototypes, and "other parts of the design process" allowed and encouraged. https://www.core77.com/posts/23506/Regarding-Kickstarter-n-Product-Design-Projects

2. **FTC v. Colgate-Palmolive standard (1965)**: Using mock-ups or simulations without disclosure is material deception; transparency about what viewers are seeing is non-negotiable. https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/defining-deceptive-advertising-through-undisclosed-simulations:-ftc-v.-colgate-palmolive/view

3. **Nothing's Concept 1 framing**: Explicitly labeled "Concept 1, a first expression of design principles"; includes disclaimer that concept design "may not be similar to the commercially available product." https://businessworld.in/article/carl-pei%E2%80%99s-nothing-unveils-concept-design-of-its-upcoming-true-wireless-earbuds-383386

4. **Wireframe visual convention**: Rough, sketch-like appearance (tools like Balsamiq) deliberately keeps work looking unfinished; avoids font, color, polish to signal "this is preliminary." https://guides.18f.org/methods/make/wireframing/

5. **Patent drawing aesthetics**: Orthographic views, numbered callouts, clean black line art on white, multiple views per sheet, no perspective distortion—aesthetic codes that signal technical documentation, not marketing. https://tuckerup.com/patents/patent-drawings

6. **Dieter Rams' drawing method**: Relied on systematic line sketches during design process; sketching conventions established at Braun codified design language. Studio published ~200 sketches alongside prototypes and finished products to show evolution. https://designmuseum.org/designers/dieter-rams

7. **University research labeling**: Projects explicitly labeled "research project," "work in progress," "prototype," "ongoing," "completed" with expected completion dates; status field required for clarity. https://www.bath.ac.uk/guides/creating-a-project-page-in-typecase/

8. **Industrial design sketch progression**: Team workflow moves from hand sketches (minutes/hours) → CAD models (days) → renders, with sketches often more persuasive to clients than slick renders because they signal openness to feedback. https://www.architectgpt.io/blog/render-vs-sketch-client-communication-hierarchy

9. **Concept car presentation**: Manufacturers present design studies at motor shows using bespoke sketches and renderings, labeled explicitly as concept or design study; not presented as shipping products. https://www.cardesignnews.com/ (multiple motor show articles)

10. **Line drawing and annotation convention**: Technical sketches use fine black ink lines, colored construction guidelines (red/blue), hand-written annotations, and numbered callouts; these visual codes signal "working document" vs finished design. https://vizcom.com/blog/sketching-techniques-for-designers

11. **Wireframing best practice**: Steer clear of color, fonts, visual details; prioritize structure, placement, hierarchy. Rough appearance invites feedback; polish signals "approved and final." https://guides.18f.org/methods/make/wireframing/

12. **Teenage Engineering / Nothing design philosophy**: Exposed components and transparent design; published sketches and design process alongside concept product. Studio showed design principles before revealing product itself. https://www.sfmoma.org/read/stay-curious-stay-naïve-an-interview-with-teenage-engineering-jesper-kouthoofd/

13. **Rendering vs sketch hierarchy**: Not binary choice; context matters. For concept-stage: sketches, wireframes, CAD line drawings signal "early, open to change." For validation/investment: renders show resolved direction. Choose based on audience and goal. https://boards.core77.com/t/Fancy-Renders/25732

14. **Visual polish as status signal**: Minimal color, sketch-like line weight, visible construction guides, hand-written notes, and "rough" presentation all communicate "this is not final." Conversely, high polish (textures, lighting, photorealism) communicates "this is resolved or shipping."

## What to Take

- **Label explicitly**: "Concept," "Research Project," "Prototype," or "Early Stage" in title and first paragraph.
- **Use sketch-like visuals**: Line drawings, wireframes, or technical drawings (not photorealistic renders); hand-drawn annotations and numbered callouts.
- **Show the design process**: Include progression from sketch → CAD → clay render; don't hide the iterations.
- **Disclose status**: State clearly what exists (nothing yet), what is being explored, and expected timeline.
- **Use design conventions**: Patent-drawing aesthetics, orthographic views, construction guides, and minimal color signal "technical documentation, not marketing."

## What to Avoid

- **Photorealistic renderings**: Kickstarter, FTC, and crowdfunding platforms prohibit them for unreleased hardware; backers mistake renders for shipped products.
- **Undisclosed simulations**: FTC rule: any simulation must be clearly labeled; failing to disclose is material deception.
- **High visual polish**: Textures, professional lighting, final-product photography all imply "this is shipping"; avoid for concept-stage work.
- **Vague status claims**: "Coming soon" or "Next generation" without timeline or "prototype/research/concept" label is misleading.
- **Hiding iterations**: Showing only the final CAD makes concept work feel inevitable; show sketches to signal thinking is ongoing and feedback is welcome.
