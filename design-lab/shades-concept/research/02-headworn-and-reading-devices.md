# How Product Companies Present Head-Worn & Reading Devices

## Observations

1. **Premium neutral palettes over neon/glow** — Even Realities G1 uses blacks, grays, whites, and accent green; Amazon Kindle emphasizes metallic color naming ("Metallic Jade"); Meta Ray-Ban avoids HUD chrome. Xreal's fluorescent logos and translucent bodies read as gaming/consumer, not premium. [https://www.evenrealities.com/G1]

2. **Lifestyle photography as trust signal** — Amazon Kindle Scribe hero shows a woman in casual clothing lounging on a couch writing; this positions the device as everyday, not tech-first. Contrast: gaming glasses emphasize specs over usage context. [https://aboutamazon.com/news/devices/kindle-scribe]

3. **Display as transparency, not intrusion** — Google Android XR emphasizes "completely clear and transparent lenses" so "people can see your eyes unobstructed." Minimal display info in "small corner of vision." This builds comfort, not excitement. [https://www.yankodesign.com/2025/04/19/android-xr-smart-glasses-prototype-looks-like-simple-thick-rimmed-spectacles/]

4. **Materials language telegraphs premium** — Even G1: "Magnesium fused with sandstone"; Amazon Kindle: "finely crafted Premium Pen" with "soft-tipped eraser feels like a pencil"; Meta Ray-Ban: "handcrafted Mazzucchelli Italian acetate." Materials matter more than circuit specs in premium framing. [https://www.evenrealities.com/G1]

5. **Reading devices show ppi, warmth, and context** — Kindle Scribe leads with "300 ppi front-lit display" and "32 LEDs" (highest of any Kindle); framed as enabling deeper engagement. Hero shows actual reading/writing, not floating text. [https://liliputing.com/amazon-kindle-scribe-is-a-10-2-inch-e-ink-writing-slate-for-340/]

6. **Prototype framing lives in developer kit language** — Snap Spectacles 5 explicitly states "$99/month mandatory 12-month commitment"; called "pure devkits, not suitable for consumer AR." Meta Orion uses "most polished prototype we've ever developed"—signals maturity without claiming shipping date. [https://skarredghost.com/2024/11/04/snap-spectacles-5-hands-on-review/]

7. **Minimal form factor over bulk** — Brilliant Labs Frame at 39g with "lightweight AR glasses" language; Even G1 rejects gadget-first thinking with "glasses you'd wear even without tech"; both avoid heavy/technical language. Snap Spectacles 5 at 226g is described as bulky and uncomfortable. [https://www.evenrealities.com/G1]

8. **Utility-forward headlines, not specs** — Even G1: "Useful. Then essential"; Kindle: "take notes...write lists...summarize"; Meta Orion: "time machine to the future." Gaming glasses lead with nits/brightness/FOV; premium devices lead with what you do. [https://mixed-news.com/en/meta-connect-2024-orion-ar-glasses/]

9. **Everyday eyewear silhouettes ground premium positioning** — Even G1 offers "panto or rectangular shapes"; Google Android XR looks like thick-rimmed prescription glasses; Meta Ray-Ban works with classic frame styles. Thick/bulky futuristic forms signal prototype/developer stage. [https://www.yankodesign.com/2025/04/19/android-xr-smart-glasses-prototype-looks-like-simple-thick-rimmed-spectacles/]

10. **Optical engineering language builds premium confidence** — Meta Orion: "silicon carbide lenses (not glass), 70-degree field of view, uLED projectors, waveguides." This technical depth signals R&D investment and sophistication; contrast to "translucent body, playful vibe" (Xreal). [https://mixed-news.com/en/meta-connect-2024-orion-ar-glasses/]

11. **Control methods signal premium or gaming** — Meta Orion uses eye-tracking, hand gestures, voice, and neural EMG interface; premium tone. Xreal emphasizes modular snap-on frames and 3D-printable designs; consumer/maker tone. [https://mixed-news.com/en/meta-connect-2024-orion-ar-glasses/]

12. **Whitespace and minimal text density convey craft** — Even G1 product pages use generous whitespace and high-quality photography; Kindle emphasizes pen-on-paper haptics over screen specs. Gaming/consumer products pack specs densely. [https://www.evenrealities.com/G1]

## What to Take

- Use **neutral palette** (blacks, grays, whites) with **single accent color** (soft green, signal red only if critical feature)
- Lead with **lifestyle photography** showing person reading/using the device in calm context (couch, desk, natural light)
- Emphasize **materials and craftsmanship** over screen specs; mention ppi/brightness only if reading is core feature
- Describe **minimal visual intrusion**; frame display as transparent/corner-placed, not dominant overlay
- For concept stage: use "**research prototype**" language, not "coming soon" or "not for sale"

## What to Avoid

- **Neon, glow, or HUD chrome** — reads gaming/sci-fi instantly, kills premium positioning
- **Dense spec tables** as hero content — premium devices lead with use case, not nits/brightness
- **Futuristic, oversized form factors** in hero image — thick bulky frames signal prototype stage; sleek frames signal product
- **Floating text overlays** simulating AR display — use subtle corner placement or contextual integration instead
- **Voice-of-marketing language** ("revolutionary," "game-changing") — let materials, restraint, and utility speak
