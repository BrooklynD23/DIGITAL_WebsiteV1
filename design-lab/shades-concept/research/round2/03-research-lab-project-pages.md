## 2. Per-page notes

### 2.1 Google Project Starline, prototype post (FETCHED)

- **Hero / headline (verbatim):** "A first look at Project Starline's new, simpler prototype"
- **Opening (verbatim):** "Our newest Project Starline prototype, which we're testing with partners, now has a simpler design to fit into more offices."
- **Research status:**
  - Word "prototype" in the headline, the first line, and a section title.
  - "testing with partners" and "early access program" describe who uses it.
  - Category tag reads "Research" (site taxonomy, not a stage label).
  - No "not a product" or "pilot" wording.
  - Placement: in the first line, in the headline. Prominence: high.
- **Section order (top to bottom):**
  1. Headline and hero image (two people meeting, one on screen)
  2. Illustration caption: "Our new prototype got smaller thanks to advancements in AI."
  3. "A simpler prototype, enabled by AI"
  4. "Early feedback from our partners"
  5. "Related stories" (six cards, each tagged by category)
- **Visual devices:** One hero photo; one captioned image; no diagrams or "how it works" sequence found in the text. Whether the caption image is a render or a photo of hardware could not be confirmed from text.
- **Motion / scroll:** Not observable (text fetch).
- **Transfer to SHADES:** Put the stage word ("prototype") in the first line, not in a footnote. Pair it with who is testing it, which is more honest than an unqualified launch.
- **Avoid:** The "Related stories" card grid, which pushes new work next to the concept and makes it look like a shipped line.

## 4. Patterns

1. **Stage word in the first line.** Starline ("prototype" in the headline and first sentence). Project Tokyo ("aims to understand"). Use a stage word where a reader sees it before anything else.
2. **Access model stated as the top banner.** Aria ("Aria Gen 2 applications are open."; "Access the Aria Research Kit"). Codec Avatars carries the audience in its "Research" section. Starline's "testing with partners" and "early access program" do the same job.
3. **Publications and datasets as the evidence trail.** Codec Avatars (datasets, reference implementations, OSS releases); AlterEgo (two listed publications); Apple (publications index with filters).
4. **Real testing photos with named context.** Project Tokyo (three captioned photos, participants named, photographer credited); AlterEgo (credited image, copyright and licence stated).
5. **Status handed off to a pointer.** AlterEgo: origin statement, then "current functionality" pointed to alterego.io. Starline's "Related stories" cards keep the concept next to other lab work.

Not observed on any fetched page: "illustrative", "simulated", "concept render", "not a product", "research prototype". These phrases did not appear on any FETCHED page.
