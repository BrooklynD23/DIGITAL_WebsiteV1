# 02. How design-led companies present concepts and pre-ship products on the web

Research date: 2026-10-09. Read-only. No app code touched.

## Read this first

1. **Verified pages: 4 of the 5-7 requested.** Framework (2021 announcement), rabbit r1, Teenage Engineering computer-1, Playdate.
2. **Not verified (official page blocked or 404):** Nothing Phone (1), Nothing Ear (1), Dyson, Sony Vision-S, Samsung/LG CES concepts. Secondary sources only, marked UNVERIFIED below.
3. **Quotes are as returned by WebFetch's summariser.** Treat every quote as "close to verbatim". Check the live page before using any wording as copy.
4. **None of the verified pages is a true concept page.** Framework's launch article was pre-ship, but the other three are shipping products. Their useful lessons are about status language, not about concept framing.

## Verified pages

### Page 1. Framework Laptop, "Introducing the Framework Laptop" (Feb 2021)

- **URL:** https://frame.work/blog/introducing-the-framework-laptop (verified fetched)
- **Status:** Pre-ship announcement. Article dated Feb 25 2021 (byline Nirav Patel).

**2. Hero**
- Headline: "Introducing the Framework Laptop"
- First text: "At Framework, we believe the time has come for consumer electronics products that are designed to last."
- Hero image labelled "The Framework Laptop" sits below the byline.

**3. Status communication**
- No "prototype" or "concept" wording on the page.
- Pre-ship timing is in the body, near the end: the page says it "will announce our full line-up with specs, pricing, pre-order timing, and a summer 2021 ship date over the next weeks."
- The article itself also says "The Framework Laptop is available in a range of pre-configured models running Windows 10 Home or Pro." This is mid-to-late copy, not a badge or hero label.
- Prominence: low. Timing sits in body text, not in the hero.

**4. Section order**
1. Nav
2. Byline and title
3. Hero image
4. Intro and sustainability copy
5. Feature bullets, then an unlabelled image
6. DIY Edition paragraph
7. Availability paragraph
8. "Made with love" image
9. Closing: "Which Framework Laptop is right for you?"
10. Footer

**5. Visual devices**
- Hero image and thumbnails only. Render vs photo cannot be confirmed from the text.
- No sketches or exploded views were detected in the fetched text.

**6. Motion and scroll**
- Not detectable from static fetch. UNVERIFIED.

**Transfer to SHADES:** Put the status line in body copy with a date window, as Framework does ("ship date" language). Do not use a "Buy" or "Pre-order" button for an unbuilt concept.

5. "personalized with magic AI"
6. "powered by rabbit OS3."

## Patterns

1. **Status in plain body copy with a date window, not a badge.** Framework (ship date "summer 2021" in body copy, verified). Also used by Playdate (season pre-order in a banner, verified).
2. **Shipping time or price sits next to the hero heading.** rabbit r1 ("ships in 3 business days", verified). Playdate ("All for just $229.", verified). Shows that status and price are read together. Not for SHADES.
3. **A sign-up form replaces the buy button when nothing ships yet.** Playdate ("Notify Me_!_", verified). Nothing Phone (1) event sign-up (UNVERIFIED).
4. **Short lowercase headlines and conversational voice.** teenage engineering ("pro on a budget.", "we made it, now you can too.", verified). rabbit r1 (lowercase throughout, verified).
5. **Visual evidence is hidden behind thumbnails with no alt text.** Framework, teenage engineering, Playdate (all verified). Render-vs-photo cannot be confirmed from text. Gap for SHADES: label every image as render or photo in visible text, not in alt text alone.

---

## Dishonest for an unbuilt concept (SHADES)

- A "Buy", "Pre-order", price, ship time or returns block.
- Spec labels (dimensions, battery, display specs) presented as final.
- A render shown without a visible "concept" label.
- A prototype count stated as company fact without a named source.
