Here is a critique from an editorial art direction perspective.

**Global Assessment**
Strong execution of the "broadsheet/journal" thesis. The strict grids, hairline rules, and high-contrast palette successfully establish an academic-yet-industrial tone. It completely avoids SaaS tropes (no soft corners, no friendly sans-serifs, no vague illustrations). It looks like a manifesto or a technical ledger, which perfectly supports "putting your name on it."

**Typographic Hierarchy**
*   **Desktop:** Excellent tension between the elegant display serif and the utilitarian monospace. The hierarchy is clear: Section > Project > Manifesto/Details. The "Build Record" tables are the strongest typographic moments—they look like genuine spec sheets.
*   **Mobile:** Translates surprisingly well. The oversized typography scales down but retains its impact. The hairline rules do the heavy lifting for organization here.

**Editorial Rhythm**
*   **Desktop:** Pacing is superb. Moving from the airy hero, down into dense technical lists, hitting the stark black inversion ("Four rules") as a palette cleanser, and finishing with the aggressive "credits are blank" section creates a compelling narrative flow.
*   **Mobile:** Rhythm suffers slightly in the "Where you fit" lists. It becomes a monotonous vertical scroll lacking the spatial tension of the desktop grid.

**Portfolio Artifact Authenticity**
Projects read as raw, in-progress hardware builds rather than polished agency case studies. The isometric diagram (FIG. 1) and the metadata tables (Build Cycle, Stack, Repo) sell the illusion that this is a working studio, not a marketing page. 

**Generic/SaaS Elements to Watch**
The only element pulling this toward standard web design is the solid black CTA button ("Take a subsystem"). It feels like a UI component rather than an editorial element.

***

### Top 5 Concrete Fixes (Ranked)

**1. De-SaaS the Primary CTAs**
The solid black buttons ("Take a subsystem") break the print illusion. 
*Fix:* Style these as stark, bordered boxes that lock perfectly into the grid lines, or as oversized text links with the arrow, matching the treatment of "See the work ->".

**2. Refine the Highlights in "Four Rules"**
The solid red blocks behind words (owner, review path) in the black section feel too much like a digital text-highlighter tool. 
*Fix:* Maintain the sophisticated print aesthetic by using an underline, a hollow stroke box, or simply setting that specific word in the brand red without the background fill.

**3. Break Up the Mobile Lists ("Where you fit")**
On mobile, the unbroken list of needs (Systems Architecture, Hardware/PCB, etc.) creates a dead zone in the scroll.
*Fix:* Introduce horizontal hairline rules between every item on mobile, or group them into comma-separated monospace paragraphs under the bold headers to maintain typographic density and save vertical space. 

**4. Reconsider the Drop Caps**
The oversized 'M' and 'R' starting the project descriptions lean slightly into "fake newspaper" pastiche. 
*Fix:* Given the engineering focus, drop the literary drop caps. Instead, use a strict monospace lead-in (e.g., `[ OVERVIEW ]`) or rely on a heavier font weight for the first sentence to anchor the paragraph.

**5. Anchor the Hero Diagram (Desktop)**
The isometric FIG 1 diagram floats somewhat awkwardly next to the "In this edition" index. The spatial relationship feels accidental.
*Fix:* Give the diagram a defined bounding box or align its lowest point precisely with the baseline of the index to lock it into the architectural grid.
